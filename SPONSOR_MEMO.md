# Sponsor memorandum

## Frontier Artificial Intelligence Officer Certification Act

**Illinois research draft v0.2 — 21 August 2026**

### Purpose

Illinois has enacted the Artificial Intelligence Safety Measures Act, now
codified at 430 ILCS 185. It applies only to frontier models trained above
10^26 operations by developers whose combined annual gross revenues exceed
$500 million. The Act already requires a frontier AI framework, deployment
transparency reports, annual independent audits, critical-safety-incident
reporting, whistleblower processes, and registration with the Illinois
Emergency Management Agency and Office of Homeland Security.

The remaining governance gap is narrow. The independent lead auditor must sign
the audit report, but no natural person controlling the developer must sign for
the developer. The Act's principal civil penalties apply to the company. Its
officers and directors receive internal whistleblower information, but the Act
assigns none of them a personal duty to inquire, attest, or respond.

### Proposed amendment

The Frontier Artificial Intelligence Officer Certification Act would retain
Illinois's existing coverage thresholds and technical duties while adding a
natural-person certification layer.

Before or concurrently with deployment of a new or substantially modified
covered model, the developer would file either:

1. a certification that, after reasonable inquiry, the required transparency,
   assessment, evaluation, mitigation, and framework steps were completed and
   the deployment materially complies with existing law; or
2. a truthful acknowledgment identifying material noncompliance and a
   remediation plan.

A similar signed management response would be due within 30 days after the
annual independent audit. An acknowledgment would disclose noncompliance; it
would not authorize deployment, establish compliance, or create a defense.

### Who signs

The required natural persons would be:

- the developer's highest-ranking executive;
- the highest-ranking executive of an ultimate controlling affiliate;
- the most senior officer responsible for the frontier AI framework who has
  authority to prevent a noncompliant deployment; and
- any other natural person possessing final material decision authority over
  deployment, relevant budgets, compute, infrastructure, risk policy,
  appointments, ownership, voting, trust, contractual, or governance rights.

Substance would control over title. A vacancy, committee, unusual entity form,
or divided authority could not leave the statute without a signer. Conversely,
title, technical expertise, system access, advice, analysis, or ministerial
implementation of another person's decision would not, standing alone, make an
engineer, employee, contractor, or adviser a required signer.

### Personal duty and enforcement

Each signer would have a personal duty to exercise due care to ensure that a
reasonable, documented inquiry occurred and to use meaningful measures within
the signer's authority when material noncompliance is found. Supporting work
could be delegated, but the statutory duty could not. Lack of actual knowledge
would not excuse facts that a reasonable inquiry or properly maintained
reporting and escalation system would have disclosed.

The Attorney General alone could enforce these personal duties. A signer would
not be liable merely because the company violated another provision. Liability
would require proof of the signer's own false statement, concealment, omitted
filing, or failure to exercise due care in performing a duty within that
person's authority.

The maximum individual civil penalties would be $250,000 for a first violation
and $1 million for a subsequent violation. An imposed individual penalty would
have to be paid personally and could not be reimbursed, indemnified, insured,
or offset through compensation. Company-level penalties would remain separate.
The proposal creates no criminal offense and no private right of action.

### Public record

The Agency would maintain a searchable officer-signature register identifying
the developer, covered model, filing type, date, and each signer's name, title,
capacity, and attestation. Protected technical and security information could
remain redacted; the identity of the responsible natural persons could not.

### Legislative request

The attached research draft is offered for transmission to the Illinois
Legislative Reference Bureau for conversion into official amendatory form and
for review by the relevant policy, enforcement, fiscal, and agency staff. The
proposal does not alter the 10^26-compute threshold, the $500 million revenue
threshold, Illinois's technical safety standards, or the treatment of ordinary
AI systems.

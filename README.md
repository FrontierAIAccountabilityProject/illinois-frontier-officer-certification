# Illinois Frontier AI Officer Certification

A public-domain research draft amending Illinois's Artificial Intelligence
Safety Measures Act, Public Act 104-0538, to require named natural persons at
large frontier developers to sign the compliance record that Illinois already
requires.

The enacted Act already requires large frontier developers to maintain frontier
AI frameworks, publish deployment transparency reports, undergo annual
independent audits, report critical safety incidents, maintain internal
whistleblower processes, and register with the Illinois Emergency Management
Agency and Office of Homeland Security. The independent auditor must sign the
audit report, but no controlling officer must sign for the developer and the
Act's principal civil penalties run only against the entity.

This draft would add:

- a deployment certification or truthful acknowledgment of noncompliance;
- a signed management response to each annual independent audit;
- public identification of the developer's highest executive, the highest
  executive of an ultimately controlling affiliate, the senior officer
  responsible for the frontier AI framework, and any other natural person with
  final material decision authority over deployment;
- personal, nondelegable duties of reasonable inquiry and response;
- a rule preventing retained authority from being hidden behind ignorance,
  delegation, committees, affiliates, contractors, or artificial intelligence
  systems;
- a public officer-signature register, without publication of protected
  technical information;
- Attorney General enforcement against a required signer for breach of that
  person's own statutory duty; and
- personal payment of individual civil penalties, without corporate
  indemnification, insurance, reimbursement, or compensation offsets.

The draft does not alter Illinois's frontier-model compute threshold or
$500 million revenue threshold, regulate ordinary AI systems, create technical
safety standards, impose liability merely because a company violated another
provision, create a private right of action, or create a criminal offense.

# Illinois Frontier AI Officer Certification Act - plain-language guide

**Guide to research draft v0.2 - 21 August 2026**

This guide explains the proposed Illinois bill in ordinary language. It quotes
each proposed operative provision and then explains what that provision is
intended to do, who it affects, and what it does not do.

The quoted bill text remains the proposal. This guide is explanatory only. It
is not enacted law, an official interpretation, or legal advice. If the guide
and the bill differ, [`bill_text.md`](./bill_text.md) controls within this
research project. Official Illinois publications control the existing law.

## The central idea

The proposal is designed to keep practical power and legal accountability in
the same place.

Illinois's existing Artificial Intelligence Safety Measures Act places duties
and civil penalties on the covered company. This proposal would preserve those
company duties and add duties for identifiable human beings. The highest
executives and the people with final material authority over a covered model
would have to put their own names on the record after a reasonable inquiry. If
they breach their own duties, the Attorney General could seek a civil penalty
from them personally. The company could not pay, insure, reimburse, or quietly
replace that penalty through additional compensation.

The proposal does not use wealth as a legal test. A rich investor is not
covered merely for being rich, famous, influential, or technically
knowledgeable. But a founder, controlling owner, trust participant, board
member, or other person cannot keep final practical power while escaping the
statute merely by avoiding an officer title or placing the formal deployment
decision in a subsidiary, committee, delegate, contractor, or artificial
intelligence system. The test is the office expressly named in the bill or the
person's real decision authority.

That is also why the proposal excludes ordinary engineers, researchers,
advisers, and employees who implement or recommend decisions but do not possess
final material authority. Responsibility follows the power to decide, prevent,
halt, restrict, or correct the covered conduct.

## The whole proposal in a few sentences

Illinois has already enacted a law for the very largest frontier-AI developers.
That law requires safety frameworks, deployment reports, independent audits,
incident reports, whistleblower systems, and registration. The proposal does
not replace those rules or create new technical safety standards.

The proposal adds a named-person accountability layer. Before a covered model
is deployed, and after the company's annual independent audit, the people with
the highest executive and final deployment authority must personally sign
either:

1. a certification that the required work was performed and the submission is
   materially accurate; or
2. an acknowledgment explaining material noncompliance and the plan to fix it.

The signers must make a reasonable inquiry. They may use employees, auditors,
and experts to do supporting work, but they cannot treat delegation or
deliberate ignorance as a defense. The Attorney General, and only the Attorney
General, may bring a civil case for a signer's own breach of duty. There is no
new crime and no private lawsuit created by the proposal. A company violation
alone does not establish personal liability.

If a court imposes an individual penalty, it remains individual. It is separate
from the company's penalty and cannot be paid or replaced by the developer, an
affiliate, an employer, an insurer, a bonus, or another transfer. For knowing
or repeated violations, the court may also temporarily disqualify the person
from exercising the same covered authority.

## Who the bill covers

The proposal uses the coverage rules Illinois has already enacted. In broad
terms, it applies to a "large frontier developer": a developer of a frontier
model above the existing compute threshold whose combined annual gross revenue
with its affiliates exceeds $500 million.

It does not create duties for ordinary AI users, ordinary AI businesses,
startups below the existing thresholds, or employees merely because they write
code, provide advice, possess expertise, or have technical access. Some people
inside a covered company could nevertheless become signers if they actually
hold final material decision authority. The bill looks at real power, not job
titles alone.

The intended line is not "rich people versus employees" as a matter of status.
It is people who retain the greatest relevant decision power versus people who
do not decide. Wealth can accompany that power, particularly where it carries
controlling ownership, voting, trust, appointment, or governance rights, but
practical authority is the legal connection.

## What existing Illinois law is reproduced but not rewritten here

The research draft reproduces parts of Sections 10, 18, and 25 of the existing
Artificial Intelligence Safety Measures Act so readers can see where the new
language would fit. Most of that reproduced text is not created by this
proposal.

Existing Section 10 already requires the covered developer to maintain a
frontier AI framework; assess and mitigate catastrophic risks; use third-party
evaluations; publish deployment transparency reports; obtain annual independent
audits; report internal-use risk assessments; avoid materially false safety
statements; and preserve certain records.

Existing Section 18 already requires large frontier developers to register,
identify ownership and contacts, and pay administrative fees. Existing Section
25 already authorizes company-level civil penalties and states that the Act
does not create a private right of action.

The provisions explained below are the proposed additions and replacements.

---

## Section 1 - name of the proposed Act

> **Section 1. Short title.** This Act may be cited as the Frontier Artificial
> Intelligence Officer Certification Act.

**Plain English:** This gives the proposal its official short name. It does not
create a duty by itself.

## Section 5 - how the proposal fits into existing law

> **Section 5. The Artificial Intelligence Safety Measures Act is amended by
> changing Sections 10, 18, and 25 and by adding Section 18.5 as follows:**

**Plain English:** The proposal would not establish a second, separate frontier
AI regulatory system. It would insert officer-certification provisions into the
Artificial Intelligence Safety Measures Act that Illinois has already enacted.

The central new section would be Section 18.5. The changes to Sections 10, 18,
and 25 connect that new section to the existing framework, registration system,
public information, and company penalties.

# Changes to Section 10 - the existing frontier AI framework

## Section 10(h) - the independent audit does not replace management's signature

> **[ADDED] (h) Officer certification not displaced.** Compliance with this
> Section, including publication of a transparency report under subsection (c),
> performance of an independent audit under subsection (d), or publication or
> transmission of an audit report under paragraph (4) of subsection (d), does
> not dispense with a certification or acknowledgment required by Section 18.5.

**Plain English:** A company cannot answer the new signature requirement by
saying, "We published the report" or "The outside auditor already signed."
Those are existing company obligations. The new management certification or
acknowledgment is an additional obligation.

The independent auditor remains independent. The responsible executives and
decision-makers must separately state what management did, what management
knows after reasonable inquiry, and whether material noncompliance exists.

**Why it matters:** Without this sentence, a company might argue that the
auditor's signature or the existing transparency report already satisfies the
new accountability purpose. The provision closes that argument.

# Changes to Section 18 - registration and the public list

## Section 18(c)(5) - identify the people who must sign

> **[ADDED] (5)** The name, title, and signing capacity of each person who is a
> required signer under Section 18.5, together with a description sufficient to
> identify the paragraph of subsection (d) of Section 18.5 under which the person
> is a required signer. Information supplied under this paragraph shall be
> updated within 30 days after a person becomes or ceases to be a required signer
> or after a material change in the person's authority.

**Plain English:** A covered company must tell the Agency who its required
signers are and why each person qualifies. For example, it must identify whether
someone signs as the company's highest executive, the controlling affiliate's
highest executive, the framework officer with stop authority, or another person
with final material decision power.

If the relevant people or their authority change, the company has 30 days to
update the information.

**Why it matters:** The government should not have to discover the responsible
people for the first time after a violation. This creates a current control map
before an enforcement dispute begins.

## Section 18(f) - searchable officer-signature information

> **[ADDED — replacing existing subsection (f)] (f)** The Agency shall maintain
> and publish a searchable list of large frontier developers that have filed
> disclosure statements. The publication shall not include the contact
> information set forth in paragraph (4) of subsection (c). For each large
> frontier developer, the publication shall include:

**Plain English:** The Agency must keep a public, searchable list. Personal
contact details remain excluded, but the identities and official capacities of
the people responsible for signing are public.

> (1) the name and title of each required signer identified under paragraph (5)
> of subsection (c);

**Plain English:** The public can see the signer's name and formal job title.

> (2) the signing capacity of each required signer;

**Plain English:** The list must also say *why* the person is a signer. A title
alone may not reveal whether the person signs as chief executive, controlling
affiliate executive, framework officer, or another final decision-maker.

> (3) the date on which each required signer most recently became or ceased to be
> a required signer, if applicable; and

**Plain English:** The register records when responsibility began or ended. This
helps match a particular deployment or filing to the people who held authority
at that time.

> (4) for each submission under Section 18.5, the filing date, submission type,
> covered frontier model or models, and whether the submission was a
> certification or an acknowledgment.

**Plain English:** The public can see which model and filing each signature
concerns, and whether management certified compliance or acknowledged a
problem.

> The Agency shall design the list to permit searches by large frontier
> developer, frontier model, required signer, submission type, and filing date.

**Plain English:** The register cannot be an unusable document dump. A reader
must be able to search by company, model, person, type of filing, or date.

# New Section 18.5 - officer certification

## Section 18.5(a) - definitions used in the new section

> "Required signer" means a natural person required to sign under subsection
> (d), whether or not a submission required by this Section was made.

**Plain English:** A person does not escape the duties of a required signer
merely because the company failed to file the document. If the law required
that natural person to sign, the person remains a required signer for
enforcement purposes.

**Why it matters:** Otherwise, omitting the entire filing could be safer for a
responsible person than filing a false or careless one.

> "Ultimate controlling affiliate" means an affiliate that, directly or
> indirectly through one or more persons or arrangements, possesses or exercises
> ultimate authority to direct the management and policies of a large frontier
> developer. An affiliate is not an ultimate controlling affiliate solely
> because it provides goods, services, financing, compute, or infrastructure on
> ordinary commercial terms without authority to direct the large frontier
> developer's management or policies.

**Plain English:** If another company or affiliated organization actually sits
above the developer and ultimately controls its management and policies, the
bill treats it as the ultimate controlling affiliate.

An ordinary supplier, lender, cloud provider, compute provider, or
infrastructure provider is not covered merely because the developer depends on
its services. The provider must possess authority to direct the developer's
management or policies.

**Why it matters:** The bill tries to reach real parent-level control without
turning every commercial counterparty into a responsible officer.

## Section 18.5(b) - the filing required when a model is deployed

> Before or concurrently with deploying a new frontier model or a substantially
> modified version of an existing frontier model, a large frontier developer
> shall transmit electronically to the Agency and the Attorney General, in a
> form prescribed by the Agency, either:

**Plain English:** The filing is tied to the deployment decision. The company
must send it no later than the time it deploys a new covered model or a
substantially modified covered model. Both the Agency and the Attorney General
receive it.

The company then chooses between two truthful routes: certify the required
facts, or acknowledge material noncompliance.

### Route one: certification

> (1) a written certification that, after reasonable inquiry:

**Plain English:** The signers cannot simply repeat whatever they were told.
Before certifying, they must ensure that a reasonable inquiry occurred. The
detailed inquiry duty appears in subsection (e).

> (A) the transparency report required by subsection (c) of Section 10 is
> complete and accurate in all material respects;

**Plain English:** The signers certify that the existing public deployment
report is not missing or misstating information important enough to matter.
This is not a promise that every trivial detail is perfect.

> (B) the assessments, mitigations, third-party evaluations, and other steps
> required by the large frontier developer's frontier AI framework for the
> decision to deploy were completed or applied;

**Plain English:** If the company's own published framework says particular
testing, mitigation, or outside evaluation must occur before deployment, the
signers certify that those steps actually occurred or were applied.

The provision does not ask the signer to guarantee the model's future behavior.
It asks the signer to verify that the required process happened.

> (C) the deployment materially complies with the applicable requirements of
> Section 10 and with the large frontier developer's frontier AI framework; and

**Plain English:** The signers certify substantial compliance with the existing
Illinois requirements and the company's own framework as applied to this
deployment.

> (D) no material information known to a required signer, or that would have
> been disclosed by the inquiry required by subsection (e), has been omitted in
> a manner that makes the submission materially misleading; or

**Plain English:** A submission can mislead through omission as well as through
an expressly false sentence. Signers must not leave out important information
they know or that a reasonable inquiry would have uncovered when the omission
makes the filing materially misleading.

### Route two: acknowledgment

> (2) a written acknowledgment that identifies each material noncompliance,
> describes its nature and extent, identifies the frontier model and deployment
> to which it relates, and provides a remediation plan and timeline or confirms
> that remediation is complete.

**Plain English:** If management cannot honestly certify compliance, it must not
be pushed toward a false signature. It can instead file a truthful account of
the important problems, identify the affected model and deployment, and say how
and when the problems will be fixed.

> An acknowledgment under paragraph (2) does not constitute compliance,
> validation, authorization to deploy, or a defense to a violation of this Act.
> A truthful acknowledgment, standing alone, does not establish an individual
> violation under this Section.

**Plain English:** An acknowledgment is disclosure, not permission. Filing one
does not make an unlawful deployment lawful or erase an underlying company
violation. At the same time, the fact that a signer truthfully disclosed a
problem does not by itself prove that the signer personally violated the new
section.

**Why the balance matters:** The bill needs honest disclosure. Treating every
acknowledgment as an automatic personal confession would create pressure to
hide problems. Treating it as authorization would create a loophole. This
paragraph rejects both results.

## Section 18.5(c) - management's response to the annual audit

> No later than 30 days after receiving the report of an independent audit
> required by subsection (d) of Section 10, a large frontier developer shall
> transmit electronically to the Agency and the Attorney General either:

**Plain English:** Within 30 days after the company receives its required
annual independent audit, management must formally respond to the Agency and
Attorney General. Again, it must either certify or acknowledge.

### Audit-response certification

> (1) a written certification that, after reasonable inquiry:

**Plain English:** The annual response also requires a reasonable inquiry; it
cannot be an automatic signature.

> (A) the third party was granted access to all materials reasonably necessary
> to conduct the audit and no required signer directed or knowingly permitted
> material interference with the audit;

**Plain English:** Management certifies that the outside auditor received the
materials reasonably needed and that no signer ordered or knowingly allowed
important interference with the audit.

> (B) the high-level summary and redacted report published or transmitted under
> paragraph (4) of subsection (d) of Section 10 accurately reflect the audit's
> findings in all material respects, subject only to redactions permitted by
> subsection (g) of Section 10;

**Plain English:** The public summary and redacted audit report must fairly
represent the auditor's important findings. Lawful redactions remain allowed,
but redaction cannot be used to give a materially false picture of the audit.

> (C) the large frontier developer materially complied during the audit period
> with the requirements of Section 10 and with its frontier AI framework; and

**Plain English:** Management certifies substantial compliance during the
period the auditor examined.

> (D) each material deviation or recommendation identified by the auditor has
> been remediated, is subject to a stated remediation plan and timeline, or has
> been identified in the submission; or

**Plain English:** Important audit findings cannot disappear into an internal
file. Management must say that each one was fixed, is being fixed under a stated
plan, or remains disclosed in the submission.

### Audit-response acknowledgment

> (2) a written acknowledgment that identifies each material noncompliance or
> material unresolved deviation identified by the audit or by the inquiry
> required under subsection (e), describes its nature and extent, and provides a
> remediation plan and timeline or confirms that remediation is complete.

**Plain English:** If management cannot truthfully make the audit-response
certification, it must identify the important unresolved problems and explain
the remediation status.

> The annual audit response shall identify each frontier model deployed or used
> extensively internally by the large frontier developer during the audit
> period. The response is a management submission and shall not modify,
> supersede, or be represented as the independent auditor's report.

**Plain English:** The response must say which models it covers. Management
cannot rewrite the independent auditor's conclusions, substitute its response
for the audit, or market the management response as though the auditor wrote
it.

## Section 18.5(d) - exactly who must sign

> Each certification or acknowledgment submitted under subsection (b) or (c)
> shall be signed personally by all of the following:

**Plain English:** These are mandatory personal signatures. The company cannot
choose only the most convenient person from the list. Everyone who fits a
listed category must sign.

### The developer's highest executive

> (1) the highest-ranking natural-person executive of the large frontier
> developer or, if the large frontier developer has no such executive, each
> natural person exercising the most senior executive or governance authority
> over the large frontier developer;

**Plain English:** Ordinarily this reaches the developer's top human executive.
If the organization uses an unusual structure with no conventional chief
executive, the most senior people actually exercising executive or governance
authority must sign. An unconventional organizational form cannot create an
empty chair.

### The controlling affiliate's highest executive

> (2) if an ultimate controlling affiliate exists, the highest-ranking
> natural-person executive of that affiliate or, if the affiliate has no such
> executive, each natural person exercising the most senior executive or
> governance authority over that affiliate;

**Plain English:** If ultimate control sits in a parent or another affiliate,
the highest human executive there must also sign. If that controlling affiliate
has no conventional executive, its most senior human decision-makers sign.

**Why it matters:** A corporate group should not be able to place formal
deployment activity in a subsidiary while the real authority remains safely at
the parent level.

### The senior framework officer with stop authority

> (3) the most senior natural-person officer, other than a signer required by
> paragraph (1) or (2), who has direct responsibility for implementation of the
> large frontier developer's frontier AI framework and authority to prevent the
> deployment or extensive internal use of a frontier model that does not comply
> with the framework; and

**Plain English:** The most senior human officer responsible for implementing
the frontier AI framework must sign if that officer can actually stop a
noncompliant deployment or extensive internal use.

Responsibility without stop authority is not enough for this category. The
provision aims at a safety or compliance leader whose role is genuinely
load-bearing, not a decorative officer who can only advise.

### Other people with final material decision authority

> (4) each natural person, other than a signer required by paragraph (1), (2),
> or (3), who, regardless of title, possesses or exercises final material
> decision authority concerning the deployment or extensive internal use of a
> frontier model of the large frontier developer through one or more of the
> following:

**Plain English:** The bill also reaches a person outside the three obvious
officer categories when that person actually has final, important decision
authority over deployment or extensive internal use. A person cannot avoid the
rule merely by using an unusual title or no title.

> (A) deployment, expansion, release, access, or operational decisions;

**Plain English:** This includes final authority over whether or how the model
is deployed, expanded, released, made accessible, or operated.

> (B) budgets, compute, infrastructure, or risk policy materially affecting the
> deployment or use;

**Plain English:** Final authority can operate through the resources and rules
that make deployment possible: money, computing power, infrastructure, or risk
policy. The authority must materially affect deployment or use.

> (C) appointment, removal, direction, or supervision of persons exercising
> authority described in subparagraph (A) or (B); or

**Plain English:** Someone may possess final authority by choosing, removing,
directing, or supervising the people who make the deployment or resource
decisions.

> (D) ownership, voting, contractual, governance, trust, or other rights
> conferring practical power to prevent, halt, restrict, or correct the
> deployment or use.

**Plain English:** Control may also come from ownership, voting power,
contracts, governance arrangements, trusts, or comparable rights. The key is
practical power to prevent, stop, limit, or correct the covered activity.

### Authority can be indirect, but expertise is not authority

> Authority under paragraph (4) may be held or exercised directly or
> indirectly, individually or in concert with others, and through an affiliate,
> entity, trust, or arrangement.

**Plain English:** Real authority counts even when it is exercised through
layers, jointly with other people, or through a legal arrangement rather than a
simple job description.

> The following, standing alone or in combination only with each other, do not
> constitute authority under paragraph (4): title, office, seniority, or status;
> professional credentials or technical ability; access to systems, model
> weights, compute, or infrastructure; ministerial execution, implementation,
> or communication of a decision made by another; or the provision of advice,
> analysis, or a recommendation to a person holding decision authority.

**Plain English:** None of the following is enough by itself:

- having an impressive title;
- being senior or prominent;
- being technically skilled;
- having access to systems, weights, compute, or infrastructure;
- carrying out or communicating somebody else's decision; or
- advising the actual decision-maker.

Combining only those features still does not create final decision authority.
This is the provision that protects engineers, researchers, advisers, and
implementation staff who can do important work but cannot make the final
decision.

> Authority under paragraph (4) is authority to decide, not merely capacity to
> act. Substance controls over title.

**Plain English:** Being physically or technically capable of doing something
is different from possessing organizational authority to decide that it will
be done. The bill follows the second kind of power.

### Joint roles and the rule against an empty chair

> If 2 or more natural persons jointly occupy or exercise the rank or authority
> described in paragraph (1), (2), or (3), each such person is a required
> signer.

**Plain English:** Co-chief executives or jointly empowered officers all sign.
Splitting one role among several people does not eliminate the signature.

> No large frontier developer or affiliate may, through a vacancy, allocation
> of authority among multiple persons, use of a board or committee, or other
> form of organization, leave this Section without an obligated natural person.

**Plain English:** A company cannot manufacture an empty chair by leaving a
position vacant, dividing authority, assigning it to a committee, or adopting
an unusual organizational structure. At least one real human must remain
responsible, and often several will be.

### Individual signatures; intermediaries do not absorb retained authority

> Each required signer shall sign in an individual capacity. A signature by an
> entity, board, committee, representative, delegate, or artificial intelligence
> system does not satisfy this subsection.

**Plain English:** The name of a company or committee is not a human signature.
Neither a representative nor an automated system may sign in place of the
required natural person.

> Appointment of a safety officer, compliance officer, committee, subsidiary,
> contractor, artificial intelligence system, or other intermediary does not
> relieve a required signer who retains authority material to a duty imposed by
> this Section.

**Plain English:** Supporting structures are allowed and expected. But a person
who retains relevant authority cannot transfer the legal duty simply by
appointing somebody else to handle compliance.

**What this does not mean:** It does not automatically make every safety
officer, contractor, subsidiary employee, or committee member liable. The rule
prevents a person who retains authority from using those intermediaries as a
shield.

## Section 18.5(e) - the personal duty to inquire and respond

### A reasonable, documented inquiry must occur

> (1) Before signing a certification or acknowledgment, each required signer
> shall exercise due care to ensure that a reasonable inquiry has been conducted.
> The inquiry shall be based on data and documentation sufficient to determine
> accurately and demonstrate the matters addressed by the submission.

**Plain English:** Each signer must take reasonable care to ensure that the
company actually investigated the facts being signed. The inquiry must use
enough data and documentation both to reach an accurate conclusion and to show
later how that conclusion was reached.

The bill does not prescribe one universal checklist for every model and
company. What counts as reasonable will depend on the circumstances, the
signer's authority, and the matters covered by the submission.

### Experts may do supporting work, but reliance must be reasonable

> (2) Employees, advisers, independent auditors, and qualified experts may
> perform work supporting the inquiry. A required signer may rely on their
> information only when the reliance is reasonable under the circumstances and
> the signer has no reason to believe that the information is materially false
> or incomplete. Delegation of supporting work does not relieve a required
> signer of the personal duty imposed by this subsection.

**Plain English:** Signers are not expected to perform every evaluation,
technical test, document review, or audit personally. They may rely on competent
people. But they cannot rely blindly when circumstances make the reliance
unreasonable or when there is reason to suspect that important information is
false or missing.

Delegating the work does not delegate the signer's legal duty to ensure a
reasonable inquiry.

### A signer must use the authority the signer actually has

> (3) When material noncompliance is identified, or would have been identified
> through an inquiry satisfying paragraph (1), each required signer shall
> exercise due care, using meaningful measures within that signer's authority,
> individually or in combination with others, to cause prompt remediation and
> to prevent, halt, or restrict deployment or extensive internal use when its
> continuation would violate this Act or the large frontier developer's frontier
> AI framework.

**Plain English:** Finding an important problem is not the end of the duty. A
signer must reasonably use the powers the signer possesses to get the problem
fixed. If continuing the deployment or extensive internal use would violate the
law or the company's framework, the signer must use meaningful available
measures to prevent, stop, or limit it.

The phrase "within that signer's authority" is important. The bill does not
pretend that every signer can unilaterally control every corporate action. A
signer may need to act together with other people, escalate, withhold approval,
call a board meeting, control resources, or use another real power attached to
the signer's role.

### Ignorance and delegation are not automatic defenses

> (4) Lack of actual knowledge is not a defense to a violation of this subsection
> when the relevant facts would have been disclosed by the reasonable inquiry
> required by paragraph (1) or by reporting and escalation systems that due care
> required the signer to establish or maintain.

**Plain English:** A signer cannot necessarily defend a failure by saying, "I
did not know." If a reasonable inquiry or a reasonably maintained internal
reporting system would have disclosed the important facts, the absence of actual
knowledge does not end the case.

This is not unlimited liability for unknowable facts. It is tied to information
that due care required the inquiry or escalation system to uncover.

> A required signer may not avoid a duty imposed by this Section through
> delegation, deliberate avoidance of material information, or failure to
> establish reasonable means by which such information would reach the signer.

**Plain English:** A signer cannot avoid responsibility by:

- assigning the work to somebody else while retaining the relevant authority;
- deliberately keeping away from important information; or
- failing to create reasonable channels for important information to reach the
  people who must sign.

**Why this matters:** The signature would be largely symbolic if an executive
could design an information system that kept the executive ignorant and then
use that ignorance as a complete defense.

## Section 18.5(f) - what the filing and signature must say

### The required personal notice

> (1) Each certification or acknowledgment shall identify each required signer
> by name, title, and signing capacity and shall bear the following notice
> immediately above each signature:
>
> I sign in my individual capacity. After reasonable inquiry, the statements
> made in this submission are true, accurate, and complete in all material
> respects to the best of my knowledge, information, and belief. I understand
> that the Artificial Intelligence Safety Measures Act imposes personal duties
> and authorizes individual civil penalties for a material false statement or a
> failure to exercise due care in performing those duties.

**Plain English:** Every filing must identify each signer and explain the legal
capacity in which the person signs. Immediately above the signature, the person
must acknowledge four things:

1. the signature is personal, not merely on behalf of the company;
2. a reasonable inquiry occurred;
3. the filing is materially truthful, accurate, and complete to the stated
   knowledge standard; and
4. the law imposes personal civil duties and possible individual penalties.

The notice makes it difficult to characterize the signature later as a routine
corporate formality whose personal significance was unclear.

### No contradictory qualifications or delegated signatures

> (2) A required signer shall not add a qualification, disclaimer, reservation,
> or limitation that is inconsistent with the notice required by paragraph (1).
> An electronic signature attributable to the required signer may satisfy this
> subsection. A signature affixed by a representative, delegate, or automated
> system may not satisfy this subsection.

**Plain English:** A signer cannot write a side note that cancels the required
attestation. A genuine electronic signature is allowed, but another person or a
computer system cannot affix the signature in place of the signer.

### The Agency controls the form, but cannot weaken the statute

> (3) The Agency shall prescribe forms and electronic procedures necessary to
> implement this Section. A form or procedure may not dispense with a required
> signer, diminish a personal duty imposed by this Section, or convert an
> acknowledgment into authorization to deploy.

**Plain English:** The Agency designs the practical forms and filing system. It
cannot use that administrative power to eliminate a signer, water down a
personal duty, or treat disclosure of noncompliance as a deployment permit.

## Section 18.5(g) - models already operating when the amendment begins

> No later than 180 days after the effective date of this amendatory Act, each
> large frontier developer that has a frontier model deployed, operated, or
> used extensively internally, in whole or in part in this State, shall submit
> for each such model the certification or acknowledgment required by subsection
> (b).

**Plain English:** The proposal is not limited to models first released after
the amendment. A covered company has 180 days to make a deployment filing for
each covered model already deployed, operated, or extensively used internally
in Illinois.

> Continued deployment, operation, or extensive internal use after that date is
> treated prospectively as a deployment for purposes of this Section.

**Plain English:** After the 180-day transition, continuing to operate an
existing covered model triggers the rule going forward. A company cannot avoid
the new system indefinitely because the original launch happened earlier.

> No liability arises under this subsection solely from conduct occurring before
> the effective date of this amendatory Act.

**Plain English:** The transition rule is prospective. It does not create
liability under this subsection merely for conduct completed before the
amendment took effect.

## Section 18.5(h) - records supporting the signatures

### Basic retention period

> (1) A large frontier developer shall preserve all data, reports, assessments,
> communications, schedules, and other documentation materially relied upon in
> preparing a certification or acknowledgment until the later of:
>
> (A) 5 years after the submission; or
>
> (B) 5 years after the relevant frontier model was last deployed, operated, or
> used extensively internally in this State.

**Plain English:** The company must preserve the important material used to
prepare a filing. The records stay for whichever period ends later: five years
after the filing or five years after the relevant model was last used in the
covered way in Illinois.

**Why it matters:** A five-year period measured only from filing could expire
while a long-lived model remained in use. The later-of rule prevents the
supporting record from disappearing while the model is still active or shortly
afterward.

### Records showing who had authority

> (2) The large frontier developer shall preserve for the same period records
> sufficient to identify each required signer and the basis on which the person
> was or was not treated as a required signer, including organizational charts,
> delegations of authority, board or committee resolutions, and voting,
> contractual, governance, or trust arrangements materially relevant to
> authority described in subsection (d).

**Plain English:** The company must also preserve evidence of its control map:
who had authority, who was treated as a signer, who was not, and why. Relevant
documents may include organizational charts, delegated powers, resolutions,
contracts, voting rights, governance rules, and trust arrangements.

This helps an investigator or court test the company's account against the
actual organization rather than relying only on titles assigned after the fact.

### Preservation hold when a problem or proceeding exists

> (3) From the time a large frontier developer or required signer has notice of
> a critical safety incident, investigation, lawful preservation demand, or
> judicial or administrative proceeding to which a record described in this
> subsection is reasonably relevant, the record shall be preserved until final
> conclusion of the incident response, investigation, demand, or proceeding,
> including review or appeal, notwithstanding expiration of another retention
> period.

**Plain English:** An ordinary retention deadline cannot authorize destruction
of relevant records during an incident response, investigation, preservation
demand, lawsuit, administrative case, review, or appeal. Once the company or a
signer has notice, relevant records remain preserved until the matter is truly
finished.

### Government access does not mean automatic public disclosure

> (4) Records required by this subsection shall be made available to the Agency
> or the Attorney General upon lawful request. This subsection does not require
> public disclosure of records protected under Section 15 or another applicable
> law.

**Plain English:** The Agency or Attorney General may obtain the records through
a lawful request. That does not automatically make protected technical,
security, or other legally protected records public.

## Section 18.5(i) - the public officer-signature register

### The filing is public, with limited protected redactions

> (1) Each certification or acknowledgment is a public record. A large frontier
> developer may redact from the public copy only information for which redaction
> is necessary to protect trade secrets, cybersecurity, public safety, the
> national security of the United States, or another interest protected from
> disclosure by law. The unredacted submission shall be transmitted to the
> Agency and the Attorney General.

**Plain English:** The public receives the certification or acknowledgment,
subject to necessary lawful redactions. The government still receives the
unredacted version.

The word "only" limits redaction to the listed or otherwise legally protected
interests. It is not a general permission to remove embarrassing or inconvenient
information.

### The core accountability facts cannot be redacted

> (2) The Agency shall not redact the identity of the large frontier developer;
> the name or version of a frontier model to which the submission relates; the
> reporting or audit period; whether the submission is a certification or
> acknowledgment; the filing date; or a required signer's name, title, signing
> capacity, attestation, and signature or electronic equivalent.

**Plain English:** Whatever technical material is protected, the public can
still identify:

- the company;
- the model or model version;
- the relevant period;
- whether management certified or acknowledged noncompliance;
- the filing date; and
- every required signer's identity, role, attestation, and signature.

These are the irreducible public accountability facts.

### Publication deadline

> (3) Within 10 business days after receiving a submission, the Agency shall
> publish the public copy and enter the information required by subsection (f)
> of Section 18 in the searchable public register.

**Plain English:** The Agency has ten business days to publish the public copy
and update the searchable register. The information should not remain in an
unpublished government inbox indefinitely.

## Section 18.5(j) - federal interoperability

> A declaration or deemed compliance under Section 17 does not dispense with a
> submission, signature, personal duty, or public-register entry required by
> this Section.

**Plain English:** Existing Illinois law may recognize certain federal regimes
or permit deemed compliance in some circumstances. That recognition does not
erase the Illinois natural-person signature, inquiry duty, filing, or public
register.

> Materials prepared to comply with a federal law, regulation, or guidance
> document designated under Section 17 may be incorporated into a certification
> or acknowledgment to the extent the materials address the same matter.

**Plain English:** The company does not need to recreate identical technical
work. If federal compliance materials address the same issue, they may be used
inside the Illinois filing. What remains uniquely required is the Illinois
submission and the named person's responsibility for it.

## Section 18.5(k) - individual civil enforcement

### Only the Attorney General may sue under this section

> (1) The Attorney General may bring a civil action against a required signer
> who violates a duty imposed on that signer by this Section. The Attorney
> General has exclusive authority to bring an action under this subsection.
> Nothing in this Section creates a private right of action.

**Plain English:** The Illinois Attorney General decides whether to bring the
individual civil case. A private plaintiff cannot sue a signer merely by citing
this section.

The proposal therefore creates public enforcement, not a new class-action or
private damages system.

### Company wrongdoing does not automatically make the signer liable

> (2) A required signer is not liable under this Section solely because the
> large frontier developer violated another provision of this Act.

**Plain English:** This is not automatic personal liability for every company
violation. The Attorney General must establish the signer's own violation of a
personal duty created by Section 18.5.

> To establish a violation of subsection (e), the Attorney General shall prove
> that the required signer failed to exercise due care in performing a duty
> within that signer's authority.

**Plain English:** For an inquiry-or-response case, the Attorney General must
prove a failure to use due care and must connect the duty to authority the
signer actually possessed. Merely occupying a prominent position is not enough.

### Knowing falsehood, omitted filings, and concealed signer status

> (3) A required signer who knowingly makes or signs a materially false or
> misleading certification or acknowledgment violates this Section.

**Plain English:** A signer violates the section by knowingly making or signing
an important falsehood or materially misleading filing.

> A required signer also violates this Section by knowingly causing a required
> submission to be omitted or by knowingly concealing that the person is a
> required signer.

**Plain English:** The rule also reaches deliberate evasion. A person cannot
improve the legal position by preventing the entire filing or hiding the fact
that the person belongs on the signature line.

### Truthful disclosure is not automatically personal liability

> (4) A truthful acknowledgment of noncompliance does not, standing alone,
> establish personal liability. This paragraph does not limit consideration of
> the facts disclosed, the signer's conduct, or the signer's compliance with
> subsection (e).

**Plain English:** Telling the truth about noncompliance is not by itself a
personal offense. The Attorney General may still consider the disclosed facts,
what the signer did, and whether the signer satisfied the personal inquiry and
response duties.

### Individual penalty range and judicial factors

> (5) A court may impose on a required signer found to have violated this
> Section a civil penalty not exceeding $250,000 for a first violation and
> $1,000,000 for each subsequent violation.

**Plain English:** These amounts are maximum civil penalties, not automatic
fines. A first violation has a ceiling of $250,000. Each later violation has a
ceiling of $1 million.

> In determining the amount, the court shall consider the nature,
> circumstances, extent, and gravity of the violation; the signer's degree of
> culpability and practical authority; prior violations; efforts to prevent or
> remediate the violation; and the signer's ability to pay.

**Plain English:** The court must calibrate the amount. It considers how serious
and extensive the violation was, how blameworthy and powerful the signer was,
whether the signer had violated the law before, what the signer did to prevent
or fix the problem, and the signer's ability to pay.

### Company and individual penalties remain separate

> (6) A penalty imposed on a required signer is separate from, and may not
> satisfy or reduce, a penalty imposed on a large frontier developer.

**Plain English:** Paying the company penalty does not pay the individual's
penalty, and paying the individual's penalty does not reduce the company's
penalty. They address different legal violations by different persons.

## Section 18.5(l) - the individual must actually bear an imposed penalty

### Personal payment

> (1) A civil penalty imposed on a required signer under subsection (k) shall be
> paid by that signer personally. No large frontier developer, affiliate,
> insurer, employer, or other person may directly or indirectly pay, reimburse,
> indemnify, insure, or otherwise satisfy any part of the penalty.

**Plain English:** Once a final individual penalty is imposed, the signer must
bear it. The company, its affiliates, an employer, an insurer, or somebody else
cannot pay it directly or indirectly.

**Why it matters:** An individual penalty would not change incentives if the
company could quietly treat it as another corporate expense.

### No disguised repayment through compensation or transfers

> (2) No person may provide, and no required signer may solicit, accept, or
> retain, an increase in compensation, bonus, loan, forgiveness of indebtedness,
> distribution, gift, or other transfer of value whose purpose or predominant
> effect is to offset a penalty described in paragraph (1).

**Plain English:** The ban applies to disguised reimbursement as well as a
check labelled "indemnification." A company or other person cannot cancel the
personal effect through a special bonus, raise, loan, debt forgiveness, gift,
distribution, or equivalent transfer.

The rule looks to the purpose or predominant effect of the transfer. It does
not purport to ban every ordinary future compensation decision involving a
person who once paid a penalty.

### Prohibited arrangements are unenforceable and recoverable

> (3) An agreement or arrangement contrary to this subsection is void and
> unenforceable. The Attorney General may recover a prohibited payment or
> transfer from the person providing it or the required signer receiving it.
> The recovery does not reduce or satisfy the required signer's underlying
> penalty.

**Plain English:** A promise to reimburse the penalty cannot be enforced in
court. If a prohibited payment occurs, the Attorney General may recover it from
the provider or recipient. Recovering the unlawful reimbursement does not erase
the original personal penalty.

### Defense costs remain payable

> (4) This subsection does not prohibit payment or advancement of reasonable
> costs of defense. It does not prohibit indemnification of a person on whom no
> individual penalty is finally imposed.

**Plain English:** The company may still pay reasonable lawyers' fees and other
defense costs. If the case ends without a final individual penalty, ordinary
indemnification is not prohibited by this subsection.

The bill distinguishes the cost of defending a case from shifting a penalty
after the individual is finally found liable.

### Prospective application

> (5) This subsection applies only to a penalty imposed for conduct occurring on
> or after the effective date of this amendatory Act.

**Plain English:** The personal-payment rules apply prospectively to conduct on
or after the amendment's effective date.

### Transition for existing insurance and indemnification arrangements

> (6) No liability arises under this subsection solely from entering into an
> insurance contract or other indemnification arrangement before the effective
> date of this amendatory Act or maintaining the contract or arrangement during
> the transition period described in this paragraph.

**Plain English:** Merely having entered a preexisting insurance or
indemnification contract does not itself violate the new section. The company
may keep the old arrangement temporarily.

> The contract or arrangement may be maintained only until the earliest of its
> renewal, expiration, material amendment, or one year after the effective date
> of this amendatory Act.

**Plain English:** The transition ends at the first of four events: renewal,
expiration, material amendment, or one year after the new law takes effect.
This permits an orderly adjustment while preventing old policies from remaining
an indefinite escape route.

> This paragraph does not authorize any payment, reimbursement,
> indemnification, insurance benefit, compensation adjustment, or other transfer
> of value to satisfy or offset a penalty imposed for conduct occurring on or
> after the effective date of this amendatory Act.

**Plain English:** The transition protects temporary maintenance of the old
contract, not payment of a new covered penalty. A prohibited reimbursement does
not become lawful merely because the policy itself predates the amendment.

## Section 18.5(m) - court orders and temporary disqualification

### Injunctions and remediation orders

> (1) In an action under subsection (k), the court may grant appropriate
> injunctive or equitable relief, including requiring a compliant submission,
> requiring remediation of an identified violation, or restraining deployment,
> operation, or extensive internal use of a frontier model in this State until
> the requirements of this Section are satisfied.

**Plain English:** A court is not limited to imposing money penalties. It may
order the missing or corrected filing, require remediation, or temporarily stop
covered activity in Illinois until the certification requirements are met.

This is not a general power to ban AI as a field. The relief occurs in an
Attorney General enforcement action and must be appropriate to the identified
violation.

### Disqualification after knowing or repeated violations

> (2) Upon finding that a required signer knowingly violated this Section or
> committed 2 or more violations within 5 years, the court may prohibit that
> person, for a period not exceeding 5 years, from serving in an office or
> exercising authority that would make the person a required signer for a large
> frontier developer.

**Plain English:** A court may temporarily bar a person from holding a covered
role after a knowing violation or at least two violations within five years.
The maximum disqualification period is five years. It is discretionary, not
automatic.

> (3) A large frontier developer may not develop, deploy, operate, or use
> extensively internally a frontier model in this State while a person subject
> to an order under paragraph (2) continues to hold an office or exercise
> authority that would make the person a required signer.

**Plain English:** A company cannot ignore the disqualification order and leave
the barred person in the same covered role while continuing covered frontier
model activity in Illinois.

### Factors the court must consider

> (4) Before ordering disqualification, the court shall consider the gravity and
> duration of the violation, the person's degree of culpability and practical
> authority, efforts to remediate the violation, the likelihood of recurrence,
> and whether lesser relief would adequately protect the public.

**Plain English:** Before excluding somebody from a covered role, the court must
consider seriousness, duration, blameworthiness, real authority, remediation,
the chance of repetition, and whether a less severe remedy would be enough.

# Changes to Section 25 - company penalties and enforcement

## Section 25(a) - connect the company penalty to the new duties

> **[ADDED — replacing existing subsection (a)] (a)** A large frontier developer
> that fails to publish or transmit a compliant document required to be
> published or transmitted under this Act, makes a statement in violation of
> subsection (f) of Section 10, fails to have a third party perform an
> independent audit of compliance as required by subsection (d) of Section 10,
> fails to report a critical safety incident as required by Section 15, fails to
> submit a certification or acknowledgment required by Section 18.5, otherwise
> violates a duty imposed on the large frontier developer by Section 18.5, or
> fails to comply with its own frontier AI framework shall be subject to a civil
> penalty in an amount dependent upon the severity of the violation that does
> not exceed $1,000,000 for the first violation. For a subsequent violation, the
> civil penalty may not exceed $3,000,000 per violation.

**Plain English:** The company remains independently responsible for its own
duties. The amended list expressly includes failure to make the new filing and
other company breaches of Section 18.5 alongside the existing reporting, audit,
incident, statement, and framework duties.

The company-level ceilings remain $1 million for a first violation and $3
million for a later violation, with severity determining the amount.

## Section 25(b) - the Attorney General brings both kinds of case

> **[ADDED — replacing existing subsection (b)] (b)** A civil penalty described
> in this Section or in subsection (k) of Section 18.5 shall be recovered in a
> civil action brought exclusively by the Attorney General. Any civil penalties
> collected from the enforcement of this Act shall be deposited into the
> Attorney General Court Ordered and Voluntary Compliance Payment Projects Fund
> or as directed by the Attorney General or the courts.

**Plain English:** The Attorney General has exclusive authority to bring both
the company civil-penalty case and the new individual civil case. Collected
penalties go to the identified state fund or another destination directed by
the Attorney General or courts.

The unchanged subsection (d) immediately following this language continues to
say that the Act creates no private right of action.

# What the proposal deliberately does not do

The bill text, read as a whole, does not:

- change the existing frontier-model compute threshold;
- change the existing $500 million revenue threshold;
- regulate ordinary AI systems or every AI company;
- invent a new technical model-safety standard;
- require an executive to predict or guarantee that a model will never cause
  harm;
- make engineers or advisers liable merely for expertise, access, advice, or
  implementation;
- impose personal liability merely because the company violated another
  provision;
- create a criminal offense;
- create a private right of action;
- prohibit payment of reasonable defense costs; or
- make truthful acknowledgment of noncompliance an automatic personal
  violation.

# A practical example

Imagine that a covered developer plans to deploy a substantially modified
frontier model in Illinois.

1. The company completes the transparency report and the assessments,
   mitigations, and evaluations required by its existing frontier AI framework.
2. The required signers ensure that a reasonable, documented inquiry occurred.
3. If the material requirements were satisfied, all required signers personally
   certify the specified facts.
4. If material noncompliance exists, they instead file an acknowledgment naming
   the problem and remediation plan. That disclosure does not authorize an
   otherwise unlawful deployment.
5. The Agency publishes the public copy and the core signer information. Trade
   secrets and protected security information may be redacted; the accountable
   people's identities may not.
6. If the company or a signer violates a duty, the Attorney General decides
   whether to bring a civil action. The Attorney General must prove the
   individual's own violation, not merely point to company wrongdoing.
7. If a court finally imposes an individual penalty, that signer bears it. The
   company may fund a reasonable defense but may not reimburse the penalty.
8. If the violation was knowing or the signer repeatedly violated the section,
   the court may temporarily bar that person from exercising authority that
   would make the person a required signer, after considering proportionality.

# Why the proposal uses both signatures and acknowledgments

A certification-only system can create pressure to sign an unjustifiably clean
statement or to avoid filing altogether. An acknowledgment-only system might
be treated as permission to proceed despite noncompliance.

The proposal therefore pairs them:

- certification when the specified facts can truthfully be certified after
  reasonable inquiry; and
- acknowledgment when material noncompliance must be disclosed.

Neither path makes the underlying law disappear. The design tries to make the
record truthful while preserving enforcement of the actual duties.

# Why the proposal names several kinds of signer

A chief-executive-only rule can miss control held at a parent company, by a
framework officer with genuine stop authority, or by another person exercising
final deployment power through budgets, compute, appointments, voting rights,
or governance arrangements.

A broad employee-liability rule would create the opposite problem by reaching
people who possess expertise or technical access but lack decision authority.

Section 18.5(d) tries to occupy the space between those failures. It maps the
people with practical authority while expressly excluding status, skill,
access, implementation, and advice when they are not accompanied by decision
power.

# Why titles and practical power are both included

A title-only rule can be evaded by reorganizing titles, leaving an office
vacant, splitting authority across a committee, or locating control in a parent
company, trust, board arrangement, or special voting right. A purely functional
rule, on the other hand, can become vague or accidentally sweep in technical
workers whose access is mistaken for authority.

The proposal uses both approaches:

- named senior offices create an identifiable minimum set of signers;
- fallback rules prevent unconventional structures from leaving an empty
  chair;
- the functional category reaches other people with final material decision
  authority, including authority exercised through ownership or governance
  rights; and
- explicit exclusions protect people who merely implement, advise, analyze, or
  possess technical capacity without decision authority.

A person therefore cannot preserve the decisive power while relying on the
absence of a conventional title. Conversely, a rank-and-file employee does not
become responsible merely because that person has a title, expertise, technical
access, or the capacity to carry out somebody else's decision.

# Why an individual penalty must remain individual

An entity-level fine can be paid from corporate funds and ultimately borne by
shareholders, workers, customers, or insurers. Even a nominally individual fine
can become an entity expense if the company indemnifies the officer, purchases
insurance for the penalty, or replaces the money through a bonus.

The proposal addresses each step:

- Section 25 preserves the company's separate civil exposure.
- Section 18.5(k) permits a court to impose a penalty for the signer's own
  breach and states that the company penalty cannot satisfy or reduce it.
- Section 18.5(l)(1) requires the signer to pay the individual penalty
  personally.
- Section 18.5(l)(2) prohibits compensation offsets and disguised
  reimbursement.
- Section 18.5(l)(3) makes contrary arrangements unenforceable and allows
  recovery of prohibited transfers without reducing the original penalty.
- Section 18.5(m) permits temporary disqualification for knowing or repeated
  violations.

That is how the proposal turns "personal accountability" from a label into an
actual allocation of consequence. The person who retained and misused the
covered authority cannot leave the consequence entirely with the corporate
entity.
